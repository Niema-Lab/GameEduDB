In this course students study foundational and representative texts from the ancient to the early modern period (around 1600). The aim is to give an introduction to the range of world cultures, their histories and beliefs, through a selection of readings from different genres such as epic and lyric poetry, myth, drama, religion, philosophy, and history. This perspective on the ancient world deepens students' understanding of the modern world and raises issues of where our values come from, what may be universal in human nature, and what is culturally determined in our lives. This generally chronological and multicultural approach helps to orient students with an intellectual map and gives them the tools to critique and detail this overview as they pursue their studies. Diverse cultural narratives are scrutinized in terms of their effect on marginalized peoples with alternative narratives. Students will engage in assessments of conflicting values, worldviews, perspectives, and interpretive intellectual frameworks, and compare them with the conflicting perspectives of our present world. Prerequisites: ENG100 College Writing I and ENG200 College Writing II (formerly HU100 Reading and Critical Thinking and HU105 Research and Rhetoric); grade of C- or higher required in each course Fulfills: Humanities I Requirement
