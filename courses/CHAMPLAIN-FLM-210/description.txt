Through an examination of the history of film aesthetics, theories, and movements, students will learn to place films in historical and global contexts while analyzing power systems, social movements, and issues of representation. The class will watch and discuss narrative, documentary, and experimental films from various countries, genres, movements, and specific directors/producers/screenwriters. Students will acquire visual literacy skills and a better understanding of film as a shaper and product of sociocultural movements. This course fulfills the requirement for FRM-128 Global Contexts in the Creative Arts, Media, and Humanities.
