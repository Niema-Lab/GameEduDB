Mechanics are the engine that powers games – the rules by which the game functions and out of which gameplay emerges. How do we design compelling mechanics, and how do mechanics interact with the other aspects of a game? In this course, students will learn about how video games implement a variety of mechanics, with possibilities including (but not limited to) variables, state-based tracking, random number generation, physics, and more. We will also examine what makes these mechanics compelling, with discussion of balance and turning, player forgiveness, “broken” mechanics and the dynamics and aesthetics of play inform our mechanical design choices. In addition to the study of game mechanics, students in this course will assist capstone students in producing their final projects, emulating the professional studio environment.
