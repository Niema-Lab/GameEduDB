Through selected works of literature, art, and philosophy this interdisciplinary course explores intellectual frameworks and major historical events which have shaped the modern world. This generally chronological sequence will give students a schematic intellectual map which they may later fill in with detail, may critique, or may deconstruct. Historical, cultural and intellectual issues that may be touched on include colonialism and cultural relativism, Marxism and depth psychology, the rise of democracy and of totalitarianism, the clash of religion and science, and how our societies shape our intersectional identities. Diverse and competing cultural narratives are scrutinized in terms of their goals and their effects on marginalized people. Individual instructors will choose primary and secondary texts which they feel best elucidate important historical themes. Prerequisites: ENG100 College Writing I and ENG200 College Writing II (formerly HU100 Reading and Critical Thinking and HU105 Research and Rhetoric); grade of C- or higher required in each course Fulfills: Humanities II Requirement
